# Ferry approach: visual evidence for April 1906

The strongest new reference is SFMTA photograph U00541, taken from the Ferry Building tower on **August 22, 1905**. It covers the final lower Market blocks, terminal tracks, horse traffic, and shop fronts. The same photograph appears in Bion J. Arnold's **1913** transportation report, printed page **140**, PDF page **180**. The correspondence was checked visually, including the vehicles and pedestrians. This establishes publication before 1931; capture date alone does not establish public-domain status.

## Ferry proportions and architecture

The HABS report, printed pages 6–8, gives approximately **660 ft frontage (201.2 m)**, **150 ft depth (45.7 m)**, and **three stories**. It gives a **235 ft tower (71.6 m)** while noting other sources give 240–245 ft. The tower is **32 ft square (9.75 m)**, with **22 ft clock dials (6.71 m)**. These are approximate documentation dimensions, not a verified 1906 survey.

Each wing has eighteen upper arches spanning the second and third stories, above a separate ground arcade. The three central lofty arches have equal height, match the wing arches' upper extent, and use giant Corinthian columns. Wing piers are Tuscan. Double string courses run above the ground story and below the cornice. The exterior is grayish Colusa sandstone. The tower has staged colonnades and an airy lantern. Original glazing must follow the 1901 photograph and 1906 film; the report explicitly identifies later metal sash in the north wing. Do not copy the 1960 exteriors as April 1906 views.

The 1901 native-resolution postcard is substantially clearer than the earlier 1200-pixel copy. It establishes the proportions and masonry silhouette. Its hand coloring is not a measured material-color record. The central glazing is visible in `film-490`; use that near-frontal frame to cross-check window divisions and ground openings. Use `film-445` and `film-470` for the approach silhouette and tower-to-wing ratio.

## What the 1905 lower Market photograph actually shows

These observations come from visual inspection of U00541. They identify visible forms and signs; they are not cadastral identifications.

- **South / left nearest corner:** a three-story facade with stacked projecting window bays, a curved corner, heavy ledges, and tall ground-floor shop arches. A Pacific ticket office sign occupies the corner storefront. A flower cart stands near the curb.
- **South / left wall behind it:** very large painted advertisements for **M·J·B Coffee**, **WHY?**, **General Arthur Cigar**, and **Smith's Cash Store**. The ad wall rises above a smaller pale facade. The lettering is a major feature of the actual view.
- **North / right nearest corner:** a low two-story commercial block with a hipped roof, roof vents/skylights, a continuous upper window row, and individual ground-floor shops. Visible signage includes **Oakland Coffee & Lunch House**. A horse cart and merchandise stand occupy the corner.
- **North / right behind it:** a much taller plain brick wall with six visible window levels and a separate ornate narrow facade to its right. Heights and frontage vary sharply; the block does not consist of identical row houses.
- **North / right farther intersection:** a shallow two-story wedge carrying **Pacific Coast Steamship Co.** signage. Its corner roof form and triangular footprint are useful landmarks.
- **Street:** setts/cobbles cover the terminal expanse. Rail pairs and cable slots follow the Market axis, with curved branches entering side streets. A few short bollards occupy the apron. There is no planted median or broad modern pedestrian plaza.
- **Traffic:** roof forms vary between boxy cable cars and a rounded-roof horse car. Wooden enclosed wagons, open delivery carts, horses, and pedestrians share the space. Vehicle outlines should not all use one generic trolley model.

The photo is elevated and has perspective distortion. Use it for relative masses, corner relationships, rail alignment, and surface texture. Do not extract exact street widths or facade heights without rectification against independent measurements. The overall Market reservation width from LOC is 120 ft; that is not the paved roadway width.

## Streetcar and horse detail limits

The 1906 film remains the strongest evidence for vehicles that actually occupied this region immediately before the earthquake. Near the terminal, `film-490` shows an open tram platform, railing, steps, wood body panels, and glazing. `film-380` separates a cable car, an early automobile, and a covered wagon; `film-110` provides horse teams and clothing silhouettes. The 1905 aerial adds roof/plan shapes, queue spacing, and rail connections.

A useful additional **research-only** view is SFMTA U00102, **January 1904**, showing new streetcar **1345** with workers and a horse team: [exact archive image](https://gallery.apps.sfmta.com/images/originals/U00102.jpg). It shows a raised clerestory roof, panelled wood body, glazed central cabin, open end area, fine vertical platform supports, steps, undercarriage, harness collars, reins, and bridle ornaments. It is not a cable-car control diagram and does not prove that this car served lower Market. Its prior publication has not been verified, so it was **not added to the reusable asset pack**.

## Remaining uncertainties and exclusions

No additional independent high-resolution prequake exterior angle passed both provenance and reuse checks in this pass. The full-resolution 1901 postcard is an upgrade of existing evidence, not a new capture. A Bancroft-attributed 1905 oblique view has promising wagon details, but the primary digital record could not be retrieved; its secondary copy was not bundled. The Henry Ford's circa-1905 Detroit print P.DPC.011645 requires inspection before use because its description mentions a streetcar loop. Modern aerials, postquake reconstruction, and the HABS 1960 facade photographs are excluded from the prequake image set.

The original Ferry was already complete before 1906, but later sash, interior subdivisions, pavement, and transit arrangements changed. A texture crop or approximate perspective correction must retain the original source ID and record its crop/rectification parameters. It must never be described as another historical photograph.
