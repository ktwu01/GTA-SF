# Lower Market and Ferry approach: street dialogue

All suggested dialogue below is **original writing for fictional NPCs**. None is a recorded quotation, a transcript of the silent film, or evidence that a particular person spoke these words on April 14, 1906. Sources establish activities, vocabulary, businesses, and destinations. They support plausibility rather than exact speech.

## Topics and vocabulary

Use **boat**, **ferry**, **ticket**, **fare**, **car**, **platform**, **baggage**, **trunk**, **parcel**, **baggage check**, **change**, **coffee**, **lunch**, **paper**, **edition**, **exhibit**, and **attendant**. The directory and newspaper substantiate occupational terms including **conductor**, **gripman**, **porter**, **clerk**, and **baggage checker**. A gripman operates the cable grip; a conductor handles passengers/fares. Do not merge the two roles, or apply cable-car controls to every horse car.

Short practical requests fit the crowded terminal: catching a boat, finding the right ticket, making change, carrying luggage, buying a paper for the crossing, clearing rails, and finding lunch. Ordinary English supplies the period tone more reliably than exaggerated Victorian flourishes. A Saturday afternoon can include commuters, visitors, errands, and leisure travelers. The evidence does not establish their proportions.

Use **Oakland** confidently as a destination at the foot of Market. Sausalito and Tiburon are documented ferry/news-agent contexts, but do not invent a gate number, exact sailing time, fare, operator, or transfer rule. Do not use “Morning Call” as the 1906 newspaper title; the source title is *San Francisco Call*. Avoid fabricated political headlines, “Extra!” announcements without an established extra edition, and knowledge of the coming earthquake.

## Crowd behavior

The images support purposeful movement: passengers flow toward or away from the Ferry, groups wait near cars, buyers pause at shops/carts, porters move with burdens, and people cross through mixed traffic. Use these distinct activities to make density believable. A stationary uniform wall of people, identical costumes, or continuous speech from every pedestrian would misrepresent the observed variety. Crowd counts remain a design choice; the film is not a statistical census.

## Approximate compass bearing

For orientation only, lower Market points about **45° true toward the Ferry** and **225° inland**. Public OpenStreetMap [way 619590730](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/619590730) connects node [1723738865](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/1723738865), **37.7938135, −122.3956799**, to node [5854920838](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/5854920838), **37.7942447, −122.3951338**. The initial great-circle bearing between those public road points is **45.022°**. Retrieved from the [public map API](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=-122.398,37.792,-122.393,37.796).

This uses modern mapped points on the straight approach axis, not a 1906 survey. Round to 45° with approximately ±2° schematic uncertainty. That tolerance is an interpretation for the minimap, not a measured confidence interval. Modern curb geometry and service details must not be imported into the historical scene.
