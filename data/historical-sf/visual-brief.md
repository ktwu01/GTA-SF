# Market Street, April 14, 1906

The reference pack contains original photographs/postcards and 16 distinct frames extracted from the original Miles Brothers film. Sources, dates, hashes, permitted use and derivative status are in sources.json. Contact sheets are in artifacts/historical-sf/. These images must be visually inspected, not treated as decorative assets.

## Scope and hook

A cinematic, walkable district covering the final approximately 210 meters of Market Street and the Ferry Building apron. Concentrate modeling detail on this coherent region. The opening presents a real archival view, then lets the visitor step inside the reconstructed street. Free walking, boarding a streetcar, controlling its movement, leaving it, and a guided ride should work. Call and Palace remain archival subjects outside the playable district.

## Historical contracts

- The LOC film was shot April 14, 1906, four days before the earthquake. Do not claim the film is from April 17. A headline such as `San Francisco, 1906` and date `April 14` is honest.
- Market Street is reported by LOC as 120 feet wide (36.576 m); keep the street-to-building ratios plausible, and specify whether geometry uses a compressed longitudinal scale.
- Use the 1905 terminal photograph for the actual asymmetric blocks at the foot of Market Street. It looks west from the Ferry Building, opposite the film's direction. Reverse left and right when comparing the two views.
- Preserve the distinctive low corner buildings, bay-windowed ticket office, masonry warehouse, roof tank, signs, curb returns, and terminal track arrangement visible in that photograph. Do not substitute two mirrored rows of generic tall buildings.
- Ferry Building must have its long horizontal arcade/frontage and tall narrow clock tower. Film frames 415/445/470/490 show the front and approach. The three central glazed arches have substantial horizontal divisions, dense glazing and ground-level portals. The side wings have distinct levels and repeated arched windows. Use the HABS dimensions and explicitly record conflicting tower-height measurements.
- If showing Chronicle, its wooden clock tower was lost to a 1905 fire; do not blindly reuse an 1890s image to restore it in April1906. Do not use modern skyscrapers or bridges.
- Film shows several track pairs, overhead wires, stone/setted road edges, shop awnings, ornate masonry cornices, horse wagons, period streetcars, pedestrians in long skirts and hats, and early open automobiles. Preserve these recognizable shapes.
- Sunlight/color are interpretive. Postcard colors are illustrative. Dimensions inferred from photos remain estimates, not survey measurements. Background facades are period-inspired and must not be represented as exact archival replicas.
- The street should be living and visually rich, but period traffic proportions must be plausible; the film's apparent abundance of automobiles included staged repeated passes.
